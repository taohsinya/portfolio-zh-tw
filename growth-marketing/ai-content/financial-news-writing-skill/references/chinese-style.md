# Chinese Output Style

Load this when the user asks for Chinese (中文). The rule that governs everything
below: **rewrite natively, do not translate.** A Chinese reader should not be able
to tell an English version exists.

## 1. Pick the right Chinese

| Locale | Script and register | Example vocabulary |
|---|---|---|
| zh-Hant (Hong Kong) | 書面繁體中文 — formal written Chinese, not spoken Cantonese | 軟件、數據、網絡、按揭 |
| zh-Hant (Taiwan) | 書面繁體中文, Taiwan usage | 軟體、資料、網路、房貸 |
| zh (Mainland) | 简体中文 | 软件、数据、网络、房贷 |

Write each locale on its own. Never auto-convert Simplified to Traditional (or the
reverse) — the vocabulary differs, not only the characters.

## 2. What to rebuild vs. what to keep

**Rebuild from scratch for the Chinese reader:**
- **Headline** — put the keyword a Chinese reader would actually search for at the front. Do not translate the English headline.
- **Sub-headings (H2s)** — use how people really phrase the question (「為什麼大跌？」, 「還能買嗎？」), not a literal translation.
- **Opening paragraph** — open with that same search phrase.

**Keep exactly:**
- Every number, date and source. Same facts, same argument, same structure.
- Ticker symbols, ETF, product and company names that readers know in English.
- Links (translate the visible link text, keep the URL).

## 3. Numbers in Chinese

- Use 億 / 萬 for large figures: 52 億美元, not 5.2 billion 美元.
- Percentages stay as numerals: 上升 3.5%.
- Say whose data it is. US data is labelled 美國 data. **Never invent a local
  (e.g. Hong Kong) equivalent** for a US statistic — if there is no local source,
  say the figure is US-only.

## 4. Attribution in Chinese

- Named source + institution: 「高盛分析師某某表示」. Use 表示 or 指出 — not a different verb in every sentence.
- Same ban as English: no 「分析人士認為」 / 「市場人士指出」 without a name.

## 5. Banned Chinese AI patterns

- Translation-ese: 「值得注意的是」, 「總而言之」, 「讓我們深入探討」, 「在當今……的時代」
- Stacked formal titles that no human writes: 「首席財務官」 when 「財務總監」 / 「CFO」 is what readers say
- Sensational stock clichés: 「股價單日暴跌」 used as a reflex
- One-sentence paragraphs strung together for drama
- Ending on a rhetorical question

**Test:** read it aloud. If it sounds like a translated press release, rewrite it.
