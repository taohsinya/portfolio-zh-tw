# Compliant Ad Copy for a Money Product

A practical guide for writing ads, landing pages and short videos for a
financial product that is **not** a licensed adviser. It is based on a real
brief a growth team wrote for its legal reviewer before launching a series of
short video ads. Names, numbers and brands are removed.

> This is a marketing playbook, not legal advice. Rules differ by country.
> Always get sign-off from your own compliance or legal counsel.

---

## The core idea: be a mirror, not an adviser

In the US (and many other markets), telling a specific person what to do with
their money — which account to open, which stock to buy — can count as
personalised investment advice. That brings licensing and registration duties.
Many money apps are built to stay on the other side of that line.

So the ad copy has one job: **show people their own numbers next to a neutral
reference point. Never tell them what to buy or where to go.**

The easiest way to hold that line is with your verbs.

| ✅ Data verbs (safe) | ❌ Advice verbs (red line) |
|---|---|
| benchmark, compare against | recommend, suggest you |
| map, break down | tell you to, move your money to |
| scan, check, x-ray | pick, choose for you |
| show, surface, measure | should, best for you |
| flag a gap | fix, guarantee, will earn |

If the product's action in your script is "benchmarked", "mapped" or
"scanned", you are describing a measurement. If it's "told me to" or
"recommended", you are describing advice.

---

## Bounds matrix: risky vs safe, by topic

| Topic | ❌ Risky (sounds like advice) | ✅ Safe (benchmark / map) |
|---|---|---|
| **Savings** | "Move your money to {named bank}." Ranking specific savings accounts. | Show the gap between the user's current rate and a **market reference rate**, as a dollar amount. No bank named. |
| **Cash flow** | "Open {card}." "Send your paycheck to {institution}." | Break down the user's **actual monthly cash need** vs money sitting idle. |
| **Investing** | Rating stocks, predicting returns, buy/sell calls. | **Holdings overlap scan:** pure arithmetic on how much the user's funds share the same underlying stocks. |
| **Tax** | "Set aside 18%." A specific personal tax number. | Show that a gap exists; "your accountant fills in the exact number." |

---

## Layering: what the ad says vs what the app says

Keep the **ad** at the level of *diagnosis* (here is your number). Keep any
deeper next steps inside the product, behind its own disclosures. Ask your
legal team to confirm the in-app layer separately — don't assume that a clean
ad covers the whole funnel.

## Sample scripts (debranded)

Rates below are placeholders. Pull real figures on the day, cite the source,
and date-stamp them — stale rates are both a trust problem and a compliance
problem.

### Script A — Savings (benchmark)

> "I kept my emergency cash at a big bank for years, thinking 'at least it's
> safe'… until {Product} **benchmarked** my rate against the market. My bank
> pays **{your rate}**. The market's top rates are around **{reference rate}**.
> I was leaving **{$ gap}** a year on the table."
>
> **End card:** "See where your savings stand vs. the market — free to start
> with {Product}."

Why it passes: the product *measures* a gap. No bank is named or ranked. The
CTA points to a comparison, not to opening an account.

**Stronger, still safe:** say the gap as a multiple ("over 400x") or against
inflation ("your savings earn {x}%, prices rose {y}%"). The inflation framing
turns "you missed out" into "you are getting poorer" — loss aversion — without
any advice.

### Script B — Cash flow (map)

> "I stressed about bills for months… until I had {Product} **map** my monthly
> cash flow. Turns out I only needed **{$ amount}** liquid. The other
> **{$ amount}** was just sleeping."
>
> **End card:** "See how much of your cash is sleeping — free to start with
> {Product}."

Why it passes: "map" is a measurement verb. The deeper "what should I do with
it" stays in the app.

### Script C — Investing (scan)

> "I bought five funds and thought I was diversified. {Product} **scanned** what
> was inside them. **{x}%** of it was the same handful of stocks."

Why it passes: it is arithmetic on what the user already owns. No prediction,
no "sell this". Lower risk than the other two — but still send it for review.

## Lines that need an explicit sign-off

Some lines are not clearly over the line but are worth asking about, in
writing, before launch:

- **Strong opinions about other institutions** ("big banks are a scam"). Punchy,
  but close to disparagement. Ask whether to keep it or soften it ("trap",
  "waste").
- **Showing specific rates or naming banks** in creative. Ask what date stamp,
  conditions and disclaimer format are required.
- **"Free".** If there is a paid plan, say "free to start".
- **Any testimonial-style voice.** If the "I" in the script is an actor or a
  composite, check what disclosure your market requires.

---

## Pre-flight checklist

- [ ] Product's action is a data verb (benchmark / map / scan / show).
- [ ] No product, bank, card or stock is recommended or ranked.
- [ ] No predicted returns or guaranteed outcomes.
- [ ] Every rate or number has a source and a date.
- [ ] Tax copy hedged: "your accountant fills in the number".
- [ ] CTA points to *seeing your number*, not *opening an account*.
- [ ] No FOMO, countdowns or guilt.
- [ ] Borderline lines listed and sent to legal with a specific question each.
