# Compliance Checklist (regulated money product)

Fill in the capability sheet first: **shipping** (claim it), **ambiguous**
(hedge it), **not built** (never claim it). On any conflict, the live website
and live pricing page win.

This is a marketing checklist, not legal advice. Your compliance or legal
team signs off.

## Naming

- Use the product name exactly as the live site writes it (capitals, spacing).
- Use feature names exactly, in English, in every locale.
- Keep a short "wrong names" list (old names, wrong capitalisation) and fix
  them on sight.

## What {Product} is / isn't (fill in)

- IS: `{one-line category}` — for example "a money check-up that reads your
  accounts read-only".
- IS NOT: list everything a reader might assume — robo-advisor, budgeting app,
  tax software, accountant, registered investment adviser, "AI investment
  advisor". Say this plainly in reviews. It is a trust point, not a weakness.

## Red lines (any one = do not ship)

- **No buy / sell / hold calls.** No "{Product} says buy X."
- **No predictions** of returns or prices.
- **No FOMO, urgency, or guilt** ("before it's too late", countdowns).
- **No self-praise claims** ("we spotted it first").
- **No fabrication:** no invented stats, star ratings, user counts,
  testimonials, or switching stories. (A transparent scorecard stated as
  editorial judgment is fine.)
- **No internal machinery** (engine names, internal layer names) in public copy.
- **No not-built features.**

## Tax and legal edge

- Tax content must hedge: **the product shows the gap; your accountant fills
  in the exact number.** Always add a "talk to a CPA / tax professional" line.
- Never imply personalised advice.

## Naming competitors

Give each one its real strengths, from public sources. Add a trademark hedge:

> *This article references {Firm} as an illustrative example of {category} in
> the {market}. {Product} is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by
> {Firm}. Trademarks are property of their respective owners.*

## Positioning: what is NOT a moat

Do **not** headline things every competitor can say: "AI-powered", a chat
interface, "uses the latest model", basic account linking, "we don't hold your
money". Lead with the thing only you can show — usually a specific, concrete
finding about the reader's own situation. A good creative shape is:
*small question → the product reads the whole picture → one blunt line.*

## Disclaimer (always end with one)

- EN: *Educational content. Not financial, tax, or investment advice. For your
  situation, consult a CPA or licensed professional.* + *Reviewed {Month Year}.*
- 繁中: *純屬教育資訊，並非財務、稅務或投資建議，個別情況請諮詢會計師或持牌專業
  人士。* + *最後檢視：{年 月}。*

## Pre-publish checklist

- [ ] Page type is review / comparison / alternatives / roundup — not a how-to.
- [ ] Answer-first opening; a clear verdict.
- [ ] Real pros AND cons for every tool, ours included.
- [ ] Our product does not win every scorecard criterion.
- [ ] Reader questions are H2s; **no FAQ block**.
- [ ] Product and feature names exactly right.
- [ ] No buy/sell/hold, predictions, fabrication, or not-built claims.
- [ ] Price matches the live pricing page; "free to start" never written as "free".
- [ ] Tax claims hedged + "talk to a CPA".
- [ ] Every named competitor has the trademark hedge.
- [ ] Exactly 3 links, in the agreed scheme.
- [ ] Disclaimer + reviewed-date line present.
