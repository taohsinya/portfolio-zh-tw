# Page Types

Pick the type that best matches what the reader is trying to buy or decide.
All four follow the structural rules in `SKILL.md`: answer first, opinionated,
real pros and cons, "People Also Ask" questions as H2s, no FAQ block, exactly
3 links.

---

## 1. Category roundup — "The N Best {category} in {year}"

**Use when** the target search is "best X tools / apps" (for example "best AI
money tools", "best financial check-up apps"). Widest reach. {Product} is one
honest entry, usually the pick for one specific use case.

**Outline**
- **Verdict-first intro:** the overall winner plus "best for {use case}" picks,
  in one or two quotable sentences.
- **How we evaluated:** a criteria table (price, what it actually does, who
  it's for, standout, limitation). This is your trust layer.
- **The picks** (one H2 each, phrased as real reader questions): what it is,
  who it's best for, real pros, real cons, price. Place {Product} where it
  genuinely wins, and state its honest con.
- **How to choose:** guidance by reader type.
- **Bottom line** + CTA.

---

## 2. Head-to-head — "X vs Y (vs Z): which fits you?"

**Use when** the search is "X vs Y", or the reader is choosing between named
options (for example "{Product} vs {Competitor}", "robo-advisor vs money
check-up"). Highest buying intent.

**Outline**
- **Verdict first:** who should pick which, in one line.
- **At-a-glance table:** dimensions down the side, products across the top.
- **One H2 per dimension**, each a real question ("Which is cheaper?", "Which
  sees your whole picture?", "Which actually gives advice?"). Name a winner
  per dimension. Don't sit on the fence.
- **Who each is for / not for.**
- **Bottom line** + CTA.

**Fairness rule:** when {Product} is in the fight, give the competitor its real
wins (for example, a robo-advisor *does* invest and rebalance for you; we don't).
A rigged comparison reads as an ad, and editors reject it.

---

## 3. Single-product review — "{Product} Review {year}"

**Use when** targeting a brand search ("{Product} review") or writing one
deep, credible assessment. Strong for trust and for being cited by AI
assistants.

**Outline**
- **Verdict first:** the rating in words, plus who it's for and not for.
- **What it is:** the category and the problem it solves.
- **What it actually does:** shipping features only.
- **What it is NOT / limits:** a check-up, not advice; no buy/sell/hold; not a
  robo-advisor or tax preparer. This honesty is what makes the review credible.
- **Who should use it / who should skip it.**
- **Bottom line** + CTA.

Never invent a numeric rating, a testimonial, or an "I switched from X" story.

---

## 4. Alternatives — "Best {Incumbent} alternatives in {year}"

**Use when** targeting "{tool} alternatives" searches. People searching this
are actively switching — high volume and high intent. Watch for incumbents
that shut down or paywall a free tool: forum threads asking "what's the
alternative?" are a strong signal.

**Outline**
- **Verdict first:** best alternative overall, plus best for specific needs.
- **Why people look for an alternative:** honest and specific, not a strawman.
- **The alternatives** (one H2 each): what it is, why it fits as a replacement,
  pros, cons, price. Position {Product} for the reader it truly suits.
- **How to pick.**
- **Bottom line** + CTA.

---

## Riding a news hook

When turning a news story into a guest post, prefer a review, comparison, or
explainer-with-a-verdict over a plain rewrite. Point the contextual link at the
on-site hub article for that topic so the spoke passes authority back to the hub.
